    01 / ABOUT

    Who is building

    I like the moment a pile of parts stops being parts and starts behaving like one machine.

    Most of what I know came from building things that had to work in front of a judging panel: an Arduino pick-and-place robot, a camera-calibrated sorting conveyor, a Tesla coil that lit fluorescent tubes across an air gap.

    My comfort zone is the middle layer — pneumatics and sensor integration (inductive, proximity, infrared, pressure, solenoid), the CAD that holds it all together, and the logic that sequences it. Fusion 360, AutoCAD and SolidWorks for design. Python and ROS 2 for control. Basic PLC and automation concepts for the factory floor.

    I'm also curious about power: IC engines, electric drives and axial flux motors. Two Tekathon podiums — third in 2024, second in 2025 — taught me more about building fast with a team than any single course did.

    02 / PROJECTS

    Things I have built

    P-01 / PICK AND PLACE

    RPR Robot

    INDIVIDUAL · AUG 2025 – NOV 2025

    An Arduino-based pick-and-place robot that moves objects to predefined positions using motor control and sensor feedback. I integrated the motors, the sensors and the mechanical gripper so the arm lands accurately, moves smoothly, and holds a part without dropping it.

    • Arduino
    • Motor control
    • Sensor feedback
    • Gripper mechanism
    • RPR kinematics
    RPR · REVOLUTE–PRISMATIC–REVOLUTE
    P-02 / MACHINE VISION

    Conveyor sorting with camera calibration

    TEAM PROJECT · JAN 2025 – APR 2025

    A conveyor-based sorting system that detects, identifies and sorts objects from their visual features. The part that mattered was real-time camera calibration and the control logic that keeps detection synchronised with belt movement, so a part is diverted at exactly the right instant.

    • Camera calibration
    • Image processing
    • Conveyor synchronisation
    • Control logic
    • Python
    DETECT · CALIBRATE · SYNC · DIVERT
    P-03 / HIGH VOLTAGE

    Tesla coil — wireless power transmission

    TEAM PROJECT · OCT 2024 – DEC 2024

    A miniature Tesla coil built to demonstrate electromagnetic induction and wireless energy transfer. Driven with high-frequency alternating current, it lit fluorescent bulbs across an air gap with no electrical contact at all.

    • High-frequency AC
    • Electromagnetic induction
    • Coil winding
    • Resonant coupling
